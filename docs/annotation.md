# Human annotation and quality evidence

This document concerns the football component. It distinguishes historical production evidence from checks performed during this local release preparation. No new automatic annotation pipeline or new human review was performed here. Source paths below are relative to the original research `projects/` directory; they are an evidence index, not dependencies for evaluation.

Candidates originate from 50 SoccerNet matches and their `Labels-v2.json` event cues. Those point timestamps identify source occurrences; humans determine the event's continuous visual interval. The released target set contains 17 final semantic types. Corner, Offside, Direct Free-kick and Penalty W1/W2 clips can describe different semantic targets associated with one SoccerNet occurrence. Historical `Clearance` maps to final `goal_kick`, and coarse Stage-2 `foul` labels are resolved using the window/source semantics. Do not rename source IDs based on final display names.

Stage 1 uses a browser video interface (`SoccerNet/platform/stage1`) to record local `t_s/t_e` boundaries. Final event and role definitions are retained in [task_spec.json](../examples/task_spec.json); complete per-sample prompts are in the example JSONLs. Shot queries change the boundary wording according to active last-passer roles. Restart events begin with the actual taking process and end with another player's first touch/control or the specified outcome. The prompt rules restrict windows to continuous standard broadcast views, ending before cuts, close-ups, replays, slow motion or loss of reliable event-level grounding. These are the final task conventions; available records do not establish that every historical annotation was independently checked against every clause of the final prompt revision.

Stage 2 (`SoccerNet/platform/stage2_tracking`) assists human role assignment with persistent track IDs from the existing PRTReID/StrongSORT candidate tracking pipeline. Annotators watch the event, identify the role-bearing individual, assign the appropriate candidate track, inspect keyframes and add boxes where needed. `is_add` distinguishes manually added boxes from assigned proposals; this is human annotation assisted by proposals, not a fully automatic label pipeline. Original dimensions and pixel xyxy boxes are exported. Model-inferred jersey numbers are excluded from golden annotations because they were not verified by annotators.

Keyframes are sampled at nominal 2 Hz. Each semantic role refers to the same individual throughout the event; a track ID change does not change that identity. Occluded/out-of-frame/unreliably localized roles use null rather than a substitute player, resuming the same individual when visible. The final prompt asks for the visible player region. A detailed historical standard for extrapolating partially occluded boxes, or measuring every null's adjudication, was not located and remains unconfirmed.

The Stage-2 `Report Problem` mechanism records Stage-1 issues as per-clip problem files. The final window generator excludes problem clips before target and distractor/count inventory construction. The final Oracle manifest has 210 removal records: **182 problem flags, 25 reviewed same-event duplicates, 3 missing usable timestamp corrections**. These are this build's removal reasons; they are not cumulative counts across historical dataset versions. Same-event collision reviews are recorded under `RoleSTVG/context_audit/same_type_collision_review`; high overlap is a review candidate rather than automatic proof of duplication.

Source-half time mappings required a separate recovery process: stream-copy seeking can start at an earlier keyframe, and historical foul extraction used a dynamic prefix inconsistent with a fixed-origin conversion. `RoleSTVG/context_audit/timestamp_alignment_audit` reconstructs actual source origin, checks multiple frames and retains corrections in sidecar manifests. Human recovery materials are in `SoccerNet/DataRecovery`. The final correction manifest has 3,516 unique records with `verified` status, including 31 recovered additions to a 3,485-event base. `verified` is a recovery state, not evidence of independent human blind review for every event.

The historic context audit assignment files name six reviewer codes: TXJ, LJB, QBW, WJC, WQ and JJH. This establishes six assigned context reviewers, **not** the total number of initial annotators, the number who completed all assignments, or a mandatory two-person review for each final event. The original frozen annotation exports do not retain annotator identity fields. Overall staffing, training, compensation and a uniform disagreement adjudication procedure are unconfirmed. Existing records support problem reporting, manual pass/fail and exclusions/recovery; they do not establish a universal expert or third-person adjudication policy.

The cross-annotation Stage-1 UI shows original annotations as read-only `REFERENCE`, and Stage 2 uses the same candidate tracking proposals. These agreement statistics are not independent double-blind accuracy estimates. Historical 355-pair selection came from the eligible pool at that time and is not proven to be an exact 10% sample of the final 3,516 population. Role-specific weakness is visible in [cross_annotation_summary.md](evidence/cross_annotation_summary.md), including second-last defender and kick-off taker; averaging conceals it. Final review is a sample of 176, not exhaustive final human review.

After target correction/removals, policy v8 constructs legal fixed-duration, half-contained context windows covering the full target. Same-type qualified SoccerNet occurrences (target, timestamp-in-window or contained annotated trim according to the recorded policy) are deduplicated and ordered to determine `same_event_count` and `target_event_order`. Current policy does **not** guarantee singleton 1-minute inputs or repeated 5-minute inputs. Oracle reuses the trusted trimmed event clip, without time-search requirements. The three-minute extension uses fixed seed `42`; older frozen settings retain a historical date-shaped seed in manifests. That historical value is not used in this preparation. New generator defaults and documented commands use non-date seed `42`; rebuilding those older windows would change the benchmark and must be a separate revision.

`bench/generate_benchmark.py` assembles the final prompt and answer from the versioned specification, active roles, exact Oracle support and context ordinal. Its production formatting functions are retained in `scripts/prompt_format.py`. Answers map original pixels to `[0,1000]`, retain nominal half-second support, insert null where the role has no keyframe entry, and shift context times to input-video origin. Do not regenerate official GT for model evaluation; consume frozen metadata. `scripts/prepare_inputs.py` only extracts model input fields.

**New release checks** are listed in [validation notes](validation.md) and machine-readable evidence. They verify file structure, counts/IDs, prompt/answer reconstruction on small examples, frozen scoring equivalence, metadata joins, path/credential hygiene and a few video probes. They do not constitute new semantic annotation, human audit, exhaustive video decoding or refreshed agreement statistics.

Unconfirmed historical facts include: total annotator count, per-sample review coverage, detailed occlusion geometry, and disagreement handling. They should be completed by the authors, not inferred from platform features.
